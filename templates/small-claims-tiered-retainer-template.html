3. Phase 1 — Scope of Services (Negotiation Phase)

Phase 1 is limited in scope under Rule 3.02(1.1) of the Paralegal Rules of Conduct. Phase 1 includes:

  • Intake interview and case assessment
  • Document review and legal research
  • One (1) formal demand or response letter on firm letterhead
  • Up to three (3) follow-up communications with the Opposing Party
  • Up to two (2) rounds of settlement negotiation
  • If both parties agree, one (1) private mediation session (up to 3 hours, in-person or video)
  • Settlement agreement and full and final release if Phase 1 resolves
  • Reasonable status updates

Phase 1 does NOT include filing a Plaintiff’s Claim, court attendance, defending counterclaims from third parties, contribution and indemnity claims under Rules 10 and 11, judgment enforcement, or appeals.

3.1 Settlement Authority

The Client retains sole authority to accept or reject any settlement offer. The Paralegal will communicate every offer received and act only on the Client’s express instruction.

3.2 Opposing Party Participation Is Not Guaranteed

The Paralegal cannot compel the Opposing Party to respond, negotiate, mediate, or participate in settlement discussions. If the Opposing Party refuses to engage, Phase 1 may end without a substantive response. Phase 1 fees are earned for the work performed regardless of whether the Opposing Party participates.

3.3 Mediation Costs

If a mediation session is held, the mediator’s fee and any room/platform fees are split 50% by the Client and 50% by the Opposing Party, unless agreed otherwise in writing. Mediation costs are a disbursement charged at cost; the Paralegal will obtain the Client’s consent for any mediation cost above $500. Mediation discussions are without prejudice and confidential under common-law mediation privilege.

3.4 Phase 1 Drift into Inactivity

If no meaningful negotiation activity has occurred for sixty (60) consecutive days, the Paralegal will send a written status notice. If no further instructions arrive within thirty (30) days, the Paralegal may close the Phase 1 file. The 30-day post-Phase-1 Phase 2 trigger window in Section 4 continues to run from the date the file is closed.

4. Phase 2 — Automatic Expansion If the Opposing Party Sues the Client

Trigger

If, during the active period of Phase 1 (or within thirty (30) days after Phase 1 ends), the Opposing Party (or any person related to or claiming through them) commences a Plaintiff’s Claim in the Small Claims Court of Ontario against the Client arising out of the matter in Section 2, this retainer automatically expands to include the Phase 2 services in Section 4.1. The Client agrees to notify the Paralegal within five (5) business days of becoming aware of the Trigger Event.

4.1 Services Included in Phase 2

  • Review of the Plaintiff’s Claim and attachments
  • Preparation and filing of the Defence (Form 9A) within the 20-day deadline
  • Preparation and filing of a Defendant’s Claim (counterclaim) arising out of the same matter, if instructed
  • Service of pleadings under O. Reg. 258/98 Rule 8
  • Preparation for and attendance at the Settlement Conference (Rule 13)
  • Preparation for and attendance at up to one (1) day of trial (defined in Section 4.2)
  • Continuing settlement negotiation throughout Phase 2
  • Drafting a settlement agreement and release if Phase 2 resolves

Still excluded (require a separate written agreement): motions beyond routine procedural motions, appeals, judicial review, multi-day trials beyond one full day, third-party claims for contribution and indemnity, enforcement of judgment.

4.2 Definition of “One Day of Trial”

“One (1) day of trial” means up to six (6) hours of court time on a single trial date, plus reasonable pre-trial preparation and travel within the firm’s usual service area. Trials exceeding six hours, or that are continued or adjourned to additional dates, are billed at the rate in Section 7.2.

4.3 Trigger Limited to Small Claims Court

Phase 2 applies only if the claim is in Small Claims Court of Ontario. A claim in the Superior Court of Justice or any other forum is not a trigger, because paralegals do not have a right of appearance there under By-Law 4. If a Superior Court action is commenced, the Paralegal will refer the Client to a lawyer; any referral arrangement required to be disclosed under the Paralegal Rules of Conduct will be disclosed to the Client.

4.4 Limited to the Named Opposing Party

Phase 2 applies only where the Plaintiff’s Claim is commenced by the named Opposing Party (or a party related to or claiming through them — for example, an insurer or assignee). An unrelated third-party claim does not trigger Phase 2.

4.5 Client’s Right to Opt Out

Within five (5) business days of the Trigger Event, the Client may, by written notice, decline the Phase 2 expansion. On opt-out, the Paralegal will deliver the file and refund any unearned Phase 2 deposit. The Client is then responsible for engaging another representative or self-representing.

Deadline Warning

The 20-day Defence deadline does not pause for the Client’s opt-out. Failing to file a Defence within 20 days of being served can result in default judgment.

4.6 Multiple Plaintiffs

If the Opposing Party adds co-plaintiffs to the same Plaintiff’s Claim, Phase 2 covers the consolidated claim. Separate actions by unrelated plaintiffs do not trigger Phase 2.

5. Excluded Services

This retainer does not include (unless separately agreed in writing):

  • Claims exceeding $50,000 (Superior Court of Justice, requires a lawyer)
  • Appeals to the Divisional Court
  • Third-party claims for contribution and indemnity (Rules 10/11)
  • Sheriff coordination, payment plan supervision, or hands-on collections
  • Insurance Act tort actions, statutory accident benefits, LAT proceedings
  • Family, criminal, or RTA matters; bankruptcy or insolvency
  • Any forum where paralegals lack a right of appearance under By-Law 4

10. Costs Payable IF THE CLIENT LOSES

Important — You May Have to Pay the Other Side’s Costs

If the Court rules against the Client, the Client may be ordered to pay the Opposing Party’s costs. Section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act limits costs in Small Claims to 15% of the amount claimed, except in exceptional circumstances. In addition, the losing party may be ordered to pay the other side’s disbursements, a representation fee under Rule 19, and additional costs for unreasonable conduct.

The Paralegal will discuss costs risk before settlement conference and trial. Consider this risk when deciding whether to accept or reject settlement offers.

11. Limitation Periods — Strict Deadlines

NEGOTIATION DOES NOT STOP THE LIMITATION CLOCK

Ontario law sets strict deadlines for civil claims. The Client understands that participation in negotiation or mediation does not suspend, toll, or extend any limitation period unless expressly agreed in writing by all parties or ordered by the Court.

Applicable limitation periods include:

  • Limitations Act, 2002, s. 4 — two (2) year basic limitation from discovery (s. 5).
  • Defence deadline — 20 days from service of a Plaintiff’s Claim, under the Rules of the Small Claims Court.
  • Specific contractual limitation provisions in any agreement between the parties.

Phase 1 does not include commencing a Plaintiff’s Claim. If the Client wishes to file before a limitation period expires, the Client must separately retain the Paralegal or another representative for that purpose.
