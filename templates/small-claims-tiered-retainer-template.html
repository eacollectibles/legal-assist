2. Scope of Services

The Client retains the Paralegal for a civil dispute that is, or may become, a matter before the Small Claims Court of Ontario, governed by the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43, and the Rules of the Small Claims Court, O. Reg. 258/98. The Court’s monetary jurisdiction is $50,000, exclusive of interest and costs.

Services Included

Intake interview and case assessment; document review and legal research; one (1) formal demand or response letter on firm letterhead; up to three (3) follow-up communications with the opposing party; up to two (2) rounds of settlement negotiation; one (1) private mediation session (up to 3 hours, in-person or video) if both parties agree; preparation and filing of a Plaintiff’s Claim (Form 7A) or Defence (Form 9A) as applicable; preparation and filing of a Defendant’s Claim (counterclaim) if instructed; service of pleadings under O. Reg. 258/98 Rule 8; preparation for and attendance at the Settlement Conference (Rule 13); preparation for and attendance at up to one (1) day of trial; settlement agreement and full and final release if the matter resolves; and reasonable status updates throughout.

One Day of Trial

“One day of trial” means up to six (6) hours of court time on a single trial date, plus reasonable pre-trial preparation and travel within the firm’s usual service area. Trials exceeding six hours, or continued or adjourned to additional dates, are billed at $1,000.00 + HST per additional half-day or part thereof.

Settlement Authority & Opposing Party Participation

The Client retains sole authority to accept or reject any settlement offer. The Paralegal will communicate every offer received and act only on the Client’s express instruction. The Paralegal cannot compel the opposing party to respond, negotiate, mediate, or participate. Fees are earned for work performed regardless of whether the opposing party participates.

Mediation Costs

If a mediation session is held, the mediator’s fee and any room/platform fees are split 50/50, unless agreed otherwise. Mediation costs are a disbursement charged at cost; consent is required for any mediation cost above $500.

Excluded Services

The following are excluded unless separately agreed in writing:

  • Claims exceeding $50,000 (Superior Court of Justice, requires a lawyer)
  • Appeals to the Divisional Court
  • Third-party claims for contribution and indemnity (Rules 10/11)
  • Sheriff coordination, payment plan supervision, or hands-on collections
  • Insurance Act tort actions, statutory accident benefits, LAT proceedings
  • Family, criminal, or RTA matters
  • Bankruptcy or insolvency
  • Any forum where paralegals lack a right of appearance under By-Law 4
  • Motions beyond routine procedural motions
  • Multi-day trials beyond one full day
  • Enforcement of judgment

4. Costs Risk, Outcomes, Limitations & Disbursements

Important — You May Have to Pay the Other Side’s Costs

If the Court rules against the Client, the Client may be ordered to pay the opposing party’s costs. Section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act limits costs in Small Claims to 15% of the amount claimed, except in exceptional circumstances. The losing party may also be ordered to pay disbursements, a representation fee under Rule 19, and additional costs for unreasonable conduct.

Outcomes & Collectability

The Paralegal does not guarantee any particular outcome. Whether the Court awards relief, the amount of any award, and the recoverability of any judgment depend on factors outside the Paralegal’s control — including the law, the evidence, credibility findings, and the decision of the Deputy Judge.

No tax or financial advice. The Paralegal does not provide accounting, tax, investment, or financial advice. The Client is encouraged to obtain independent advice on any tax or financial consequences of a settlement, judgment, debt forgiveness, structured payment, or business-loss claim.

Limitation Periods — Negotiation Does Not Stop the Clock

Ontario law sets strict deadlines for civil claims. Participation in negotiation or mediation does not suspend, toll, or extend any limitation period unless expressly agreed in writing by all parties or ordered by the Court.

Applicable limitation periods include: Limitations Act, 2002, s. 4 — two (2) year basic limitation from discovery (s. 5); 20-day Defence deadline from service of a Plaintiff’s Claim; and any contractual limitation provision between the parties.

Disbursements

Disbursements (court filing fees, process service, witness attendance money, mediator fees, transcripts, expert fees, sheriff fees, travel) are billed at cost in addition to legal fees. The Paralegal will obtain the Client’s consent before any single disbursement above $250. HST is charged on legal fees and taxable disbursements; HST-exempt court filing fees are passed through without HST.

6. Client Responsibilities & Evidence

Client Responsibilities

  • Provide complete, accurate, truthful information and all relevant documents
  • Respond to requests for instructions within five (5) business days
  • Provide identification under By-Law 7.1
  • Attend settlement conferences and trial in person, as required by the Rules
  • Keep contact information current
  • Not contact the opposing party directly after the demand letter is sent
  • Pay fees, disbursements, and trust top-ups when due

Evidence Preservation

The Client agrees not to destroy, delete, alter, or dispose of potentially relevant evidence, including text messages, emails, photographs, recordings, voicemails, invoices, contracts, receipts, repair records, or social media content. Destruction of relevant evidence can result in adverse inferences, costs, or other sanctions.

Witnesses & Subpoenas

The Client is responsible for identifying witnesses and ensuring their availability. Non-party witnesses are entitled to attendance money under Rule 18 of O. Reg. 258/98 (currently $50/day plus reasonable travel and accommodation). The Paralegal will prepare and serve a Summons to Witness (Form 18A) on instruction; the cost is a disbursement. Expert reports and testimony require Client approval and a separate written estimate.

Settlement Conference & Trial Attendance

The Rules require the parties to attend the settlement conference in person and to be available for trial. Failure to attend may result in dismissal, judgment against the Client, or costs.

Client Conduct

The Client agrees not to contact the opposing party directly, undermine the legal strategy, conceal documents, take unilateral steps that affect the matter, or post online about the dispute. Serious misconduct may result in withdrawal.
