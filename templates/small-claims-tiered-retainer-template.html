3. Phase 1 — Scope of Services & Fees

Phase 1 is limited in scope under Rule 3.02(1.1) of the Paralegal Rules of Conduct. Phase 1 includes intake interview and case assessment, document review and legal research, one (1) formal demand or response letter on firm letterhead, up to three (3) follow-up communications with the Opposing Party, up to two (2) rounds of settlement negotiation, if both parties agree one (1) private mediation session (up to 3 hours, in-person or video), settlement agreement and full and final release if Phase 1 resolves, and reasonable status updates.

Phase 1 does NOT include filing a Plaintiff’s Claim, court attendance, defending counterclaims from third parties, contribution and indemnity claims under Rules 10 and 11, judgment enforcement, or appeals.

Settlement Authority & Opposing Party Participation

The Client retains sole authority to accept or reject any settlement offer. The Paralegal will communicate every offer received and act only on the Client’s express instruction. The Paralegal cannot compel the Opposing Party to respond, negotiate, mediate, or participate in settlement discussions. Phase 1 fees are earned for the work performed regardless of whether the Opposing Party participates.

Mediation Costs & Phase 1 Drift

If a mediation session is held, the mediator’s fee and any room/platform fees are split 50% by the Client and 50% by the Opposing Party, unless agreed otherwise. Mediation costs are a disbursement charged at cost; consent is required for any mediation cost above $500. If no meaningful negotiation activity has occurred for sixty (60) consecutive days, the Paralegal will send a written status notice; if no further instructions arrive within thirty (30) days, the Paralegal may close the Phase 1 file. The 30-day post-Phase-1 Phase 2 trigger window in §4 continues to run from that closure date.

4. Phase 2 — Auto-Expansion If the Client Is Sued

Trigger

If, during the active period of Phase 1 (or within thirty (30) days after Phase 1 ends), the Opposing Party (or any person related to or claiming through them) commences a Plaintiff’s Claim in the Small Claims Court of Ontario against the Client arising out of the matter in §2, this retainer automatically expands to include Phase 2 services. The Client agrees to notify the Paralegal within five (5) business days of becoming aware of the Trigger Event.

Services Included in Phase 2

Review of the Plaintiff’s Claim and attachments; preparation and filing of the Defence (Form 9A) within the 20-day deadline; preparation and filing of a Defendant’s Claim (counterclaim) arising out of the same matter, if instructed; service of pleadings under O. Reg. 258/98 Rule 8; preparation for and attendance at the Settlement Conference (Rule 13); preparation for and attendance at up to one (1) day of trial; continuing settlement negotiation throughout Phase 2; drafting a settlement agreement and release if Phase 2 resolves.

One Day of Trial

“One (1) day of trial” means up to six (6) hours of court time on a single trial date, plus reasonable pre-trial preparation and travel within the firm’s usual service area. Trials exceeding six hours, or that are continued or adjourned to additional dates, are billed at $1,000.00 + HST per additional half-day or part thereof.

Trigger Limited to Small Claims; Limited to Named Opposing Party

Phase 2 applies only if the claim is in Small Claims Court of Ontario. A claim in the Superior Court of Justice or any other forum is not a trigger, because paralegals do not have a right of appearance there under By-Law 4. Phase 2 also applies only where the claim is commenced by the named Opposing Party (or a party related to or claiming through them — for example, an insurer or assignee). An unrelated third-party claim does not trigger Phase 2.

Client’s Right to Opt Out

Within five (5) business days of the Trigger Event, the Client may, by written notice, decline the Phase 2 expansion. On opt-out, the Paralegal will deliver the file and refund any unearned Phase 2 deposit.

Deadline Warning

The 20-day Defence deadline does not pause for the Client’s opt-out. Failing to file a Defence within 20 days of being served can result in default judgment.

5. Costs Risk, Outcomes, Limitations & Disbursements

Important — You May Have to Pay the Other Side’s Costs

If the Court rules against the Client, the Client may be ordered to pay the Opposing Party’s costs. Section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act limits costs in Small Claims to 15% of the amount claimed, except in exceptional circumstances. In addition, the losing party may be ordered to pay disbursements, a representation fee under Rule 19, and additional costs for unreasonable conduct.

Outcomes, Collectability & No Tax/Financial Advice

The Paralegal does not guarantee any particular outcome. Whether the Court awards relief, the amount of any award, and the recoverability of any judgment depend on factors outside the Paralegal’s control. Outcomes depend on the law, the evidence, credibility findings, and the decision of the Deputy Judge.

No tax or financial advice. The Paralegal does not provide accounting, tax, investment, or financial advice. The Client is encouraged to obtain independent advice on tax or financial consequences of any settlement, judgment, debt forgiveness, structured payment, or business-loss claim.

Limitation Periods — Negotiation Does Not Stop the Clock

Ontario law sets strict deadlines for civil claims. Participation in negotiation or mediation does not suspend, toll, or extend any limitation period unless expressly agreed in writing by all parties or ordered by the Court.

Applicable limitation periods include: Limitations Act, 2002, s. 4 — two (2) year basic limitation from discovery (s. 5); 20-day Defence deadline from service of a Plaintiff’s Claim under the Rules of the Small Claims Court; specific contractual limitation provisions in any agreement between the parties. Phase 1 does not include commencing a Plaintiff’s Claim; if the Client wishes to file before a limitation period expires, the Client must separately retain the Paralegal or another representative.

Disbursements

Disbursements (court filing fees, process service, witness attendance money, mediator fees, transcripts, expert fees, sheriff fees, travel) are billed at cost in addition to legal fees. The Paralegal will obtain the Client’s consent before any single disbursement above $250. HST is charged on legal fees and taxable disbursements; HST-exempt court filing fees are passed through without HST.

Evidence Preservation, Witnesses & Subpoenas

The Client agrees not to destroy, delete, alter, or dispose of potentially relevant evidence, including text messages, emails, photographs, recordings, voicemails, invoices, contracts, receipts, repair records, or social media content. Destruction of relevant evidence can result in adverse inferences, costs against the Client, or other sanctions. The Client is responsible for identifying witnesses and ensuring their availability. The Rules of the Small Claims Court require the parties to attend the settlement conference in person and to be available for trial. Failure to attend may result in dismissal, judgment against the Client, or costs.

Client Conduct & Public Statements

The Client agrees not to contact the Opposing Party directly, undermine the legal strategy, conceal documents, take unilateral steps that affect the matter, or post online about the dispute. Serious misconduct may result in withdrawal.
