2. Scope of Services — What This Retainer Covers

This retainer is expressly limited in scope as permitted by Rule 3.02(1.1) of the Paralegal Rules of Conduct. The services provided are restricted to items listed in the "Included" column below. Anything in the "Not Included" column is not part of this retainer and requires a separate written agreement.

Included in this Retainer

  • One (1) intake interview and case-assessment meeting
  • Review of documents provided by the Client (receipts, warranty terms, photos, prior correspondence, repair records)
  • Legal research on applicable Ontario warranty law (Sale of Goods Act; Consumer Protection Act, 2002; common law)
  • Drafting one (1) formal demand / notice letter on firm letterhead, sent to the opposing party
  • Up to three (3) follow-up communications (telephone calls, letters or emails) with the opposing party or their representative
  • Up to two (2) rounds of settlement negotiation on the Client's behalf
  • Drafting a settlement agreement and full and final release if a settlement is reached within scope
  • Reasonable status updates to the Client during the active period of this retainer

NOT Included in this Retainer

  • Representation at any court appearance, settlement conference, mediation, hearing, or trial
  • Examinations for discovery, motions, or interlocutory steps
  • Defending any counterclaim or third-party claim
  • Enforcement of any judgment or settlement (garnishment, writ of seizure, etc.)
  • Appeals or judicial review
  • Complaint to any regulator (Consumer Protection Ontario, Better Business Bureau, manufacturer ombuds, etc.)
  • Communications with insurers, sureties, or parties other than the named opposing party

2.1 Settlement Authority

The Client retains sole authority to accept or reject any settlement offer. The Paralegal will:

  • Promptly communicate to the Client every settlement offer, counter-offer, or material proposal received from the opposing party, in writing where practicable;
  • Provide the Client with the Paralegal's professional opinion on each offer (strengths, weaknesses, comparable outcomes);
  • Make, accept, reject, or counter an offer only on the Client's express written or recorded instruction;
  • Not bind the Client to any settlement, release, payment plan, or non-disclosure obligation without first obtaining specific instructions for the particular term.

If the Client wishes to give the Paralegal advance authority to settle within a specified dollar range, the Client and the Paralegal will record that authority in writing (by email exchange or a written instruction sheet) and the Paralegal will operate strictly within those parameters.

If matters fall outside this scope

If negotiation does not resolve the dispute and the Client wishes to commence proceedings, defend a counterclaim, or take any step outside this retainer's scope, the Client must enter into a separate written retainer agreement before the firm performs any such work. The flat fee under this retainer does not include any such services and will not be credited against fees for further work.

3. Limitation Periods — Time Limits on the Client's Rights

Important: Negotiation Does Not Stop the Clock

Ontario law sets strict deadlines (called limitation periods) to start a legal claim. Engaging in negotiation, sending a demand letter, or waiting for the opposing party to respond does not pause or extend these deadlines.

The principal limitation periods that may apply include:

  • Limitations Act, 2002, s. 4 — a two (2) year basic limitation period running from the date the claim was, or ought reasonably to have been, discovered (s. 5 discoverability).
  • Consumer Protection Act, 2002, s. 96 — written notice of rescission of a consumer agreement must generally be given within one (1) year, depending on the ground.
  • Specific contractual or warranty notice provisions in the goods or services agreement itself.

The Paralegal will identify the applicable limitation period(s) as part of the intake assessment, and the Client agrees to keep track of any deadline communicated. If a limitation period is approaching, the Client must promptly retain counsel (this firm or another) to commence proceedings; this retainer does not include commencing proceedings.

6. Withdrawal of Services

6.1 By the Client

The Client may terminate this retainer at any time on written notice. The Paralegal will, on termination, prepare a final account, refund any unearned balance held in trust, and return the Client's original documents per Section 11.

6.2 By the Paralegal

Consistent with Rule 3.08 of the Paralegal Rules of Conduct, the Paralegal may withdraw from this retainer on reasonable written notice if:

  • The Client fails to pay fees or disbursements when due;
  • A material conflict of interest arises that cannot be resolved with informed consent;
  • The Client provides instructions the Paralegal cannot ethically carry out (for example, instructions to mislead the opposing party, withhold material facts, or make a representation the Paralegal knows to be false);
  • The trust relationship between the Client and the Paralegal has broken down such that the Paralegal can no longer effectively represent the Client;
  • The Client persistently fails to communicate or provide instructions despite reasonable requests.

Where the Paralegal withdraws, the Paralegal will give reasonable notice, complete steps reasonably required to protect the Client's interests in the short term, account for any trust money held, and return the Client's documents per Section 11.

6.3 Refund on Withdrawal

On termination by either party, refund of the flat fee is calculated as follows:

  • Demand letter not yet sent: refund of 50% of the flat fee (less HST already remitted and any disbursements incurred);
  • Demand letter sent but follow-up communications not exhausted: refund of 25% of the flat fee (less HST and disbursements);
  • Demand letter sent and all follow-up communications and negotiation rounds completed: no refund payable.
