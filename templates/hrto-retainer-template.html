4. Scope of Services

Subject to Section 5, our services include:

  • Reviewing the facts and assessing whether they engage one or more grounds protected by the Ontario Human Rights Code;
  • Drafting and filing an Application (Form 1) with the HRTO under s. 34 of the Code, or filing a Response (Form 2) on behalf of a respondent within the 35‑day deadline after service;
  • Drafting an applicant’s Reply (Form 3) within 21 days of receiving the Response, where appropriate;
  • Preparing and exchanging disclosure of documents under HRTO Rule 16 and witness statements under Rule 17;
  • Drafting and responding to routine procedural motions, scheduling correspondence, adjournment requests, and accommodation requests;
  • Representing the Client at mediation (scheduled in every application as of June 1, 2025 under Rule 15) and at any Case Assessment Direction (CAD) or summary hearing under Rule 19A;
  • Drafting and reviewing settlement offers, Minutes of Settlement, and Form 25 Confirmation of Settlement;
  • Representing the Client at the merits hearing, including examination‑in‑chief, cross‑examination, and submissions;
  • Receiving and explaining the Tribunal’s decision and any remedy ordered under s. 45.2 of the Code.

13. Limitation Period

Strict One-Year Limit

Under s. 34(1) of the Ontario Human Rights Code, an Application must be filed within one year of the alleged incident — or, where there is a series of incidents, within one year of the last incident in the series. The Tribunal may extend this period under s. 34(2) only where the delay was in good faith and no substantial prejudice will result, which is a high threshold and is not granted as a matter of course.

The Client confirms that they have advised the Paralegal of all relevant dates and that the Application can be filed within the limitation period, or that the Client is aware of the risks of seeking an extension.

14. Mediation Confidentiality

Under HRTO Rule 15, anything said, written, or produced during mediation is confidential and without prejudice. The parties, their representatives, and any intervenors or affected persons present at mediation must sign the Tribunal’s confidentiality agreement before the mediation begins. Matters disclosed during mediation may not be raised before the Tribunal or in other proceedings except (a) a final, written, signed settlement agreement, or (b) where disclosure is otherwise required by law.

Effective June 1, 2025, the HRTO schedules mediation in every application as standard practice. The Client may request an exemption only in exceptional circumstances.

15. Tribunal Scheduling, Delay & No Guaranteed Outcome

The Paralegal has not made and cannot make any promise, prediction, or guarantee about the outcome of the Client’s HRTO matter. Past results in similar cases do not predict future results. The Tribunal retains sole discretion regarding credibility findings, admissibility of evidence, procedural rulings, and the remedies (if any) ordered under s. 45.2 of the Code.

The Client expressly acknowledges that the Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario exclusively controls scheduling, mediation dates, hearing dates, and the release of decisions. The Client acknowledges that HRTO proceedings frequently take many months or years to conclude due to Tribunal scheduling, backlog, adjournments, and procedural delays that are beyond the Paralegal’s control.
