2. Retainer & Relationship

The Client retains the Paralegal to represent the Client before the Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario (the "HRTO" or the "Tribunal") in respect of the matter described in Section 1. The Paralegal accepts the retainer subject to the terms of this Agreement.

The Paralegal acts only within the scope of practice authorized by By-Law 4 of the Law Society of Ontario for paralegals. Nothing in this Agreement creates a solicitor-client relationship with a lawyer or an obligation on us to provide legal services that fall outside paralegal scope.

3. Scope of Services

Subject to Section 4, our services include:

  • Reviewing the facts and assessing whether they engage one or more grounds protected by the Ontario Human Rights Code;
  • Drafting and filing an Application (Form 1) with the HRTO, or responding to an Application as Respondent (Form 2);
  • Drafting a Reply (Form 3) where appropriate;
  • Preparing and exchanging mandatory disclosure under HRTO Rule 16;
  • Representing the Client at mediation and at any Case Assessment Direction (CAD) or summary hearing under Rule 19A;
  • Drafting and reviewing settlement offers and Minutes of Settlement;
  • Representing the Client at the merits hearing, including examination-in-chief, cross-examination, and submissions;
  • Receiving and explaining the Tribunal's decision and any remedy ordered under s. 45.2 of the Code.

Protected grounds alleged under s. 1 of the Code

  • Race
  • Ancestry
  • Place of origin
  • Colour
  • Ethnic origin
  • Citizenship
  • Creed
  • Sex (incl. pregnancy)
  • Sexual orientation
  • Gender identity
  • Gender expression
  • Age
  • Marital status
  • Family status
  • Disability
  • Receipt of public assistance (housing)
  • Record of offences (employment)
  • Association with a protected person

4. Excluded Services

This retainer does not include, and we are not authorized to provide:

  • Appearances before the Divisional Court or any other court (paralegals do not have rights of audience in Superior Court of Justice or Divisional Court);
  • Applications for judicial review or appeals to the Divisional Court;
  • Civil claims for damages outside the HRTO's jurisdiction (e.g. wrongful dismissal at common law beyond the HRTO's remedial reach);
  • Federal human rights matters under the Canadian Human Rights Act;
  • Criminal, family, immigration, or estates matters;
  • Tax or accounting advice;
  • Negotiation or drafting of agreements unrelated to the HRTO matter.

8. Evidence & Disclosure

Under HRTO Rule 16 and Rule 17, each party must disclose to the other party and to the Tribunal a list of documents and witnesses relevant to the application. The Client must preserve all communications, records, and evidence in their possession and produce them to us promptly. Failure to preserve evidence (whether paper or electronic — including text messages, social media posts, and emails) may result in adverse inferences being drawn by the Tribunal.

9. Limitation Period

Strict one-year limit

Under s. 34(1) of the Ontario Human Rights Code, an Application must be filed within one year of the alleged incident — or, where there is a series of incidents, within one year of the last incident in the series. The Tribunal may extend this period under s. 34(2) only where the delay was in good faith and no substantial prejudice will result, which is a high threshold and is not granted as a matter of course.

The Client confirms that they have advised the Paralegal of all relevant dates and that the Application can be filed within the limitation period, or that the Client is aware of the risks of seeking an extension.

10. Mediation Confidentiality

Under HRTO Rule 17, anything said, written, or produced during mediation is confidential and without prejudice. Neither party may rely on or refer to mediation discussions in subsequent proceedings, except (a) a final, written, signed settlement agreement, or (b) where disclosure is required by law.

11. Client Conduct

The Client agrees to behave courteously toward the Paralegal, Tribunal staff, mediators, witnesses, opposing parties, and opposing counsel. Threatening, harassing, or abusive conduct directed at any of the foregoing — whether in person, by email, by phone, or on social media — is grounds for the Paralegal to withdraw from the retainer under Section 19.

12. No Guaranteed Outcome

The Paralegal has not made and cannot make any promise, prediction, or guarantee about the outcome of the Client's HRTO matter. Past results in similar cases do not predict future results. Decisions of the Tribunal turn on the specific facts, evidence, and credibility findings of each case.

13. Referral & Other Counsel

The Client may consult or retain a lawyer at any time. The Paralegal may, where appropriate and with the Client's consent, refer the Client to a lawyer (for example, where the matter exceeds paralegal scope, or where parallel Superior Court proceedings are advisable). Any referral is made in the Client's best interests and not for any referral fee.

14. Parallel Proceedings

Under s. 34(11) of the Code, the HRTO will not deal with an application that is the subject of a civil proceeding seeking the same remedy. The Client must inform the Paralegal of any other proceeding — civil, criminal, regulatory, or before another tribunal — that arises out of the same facts. The Client also acknowledges that filing an HRTO application may affect, or be affected by, parallel proceedings such as Employment Standards complaints, WSIB claims, or civil actions.

16. File Assignment

17. Social Media

Do not post about the case

The Client agrees not to post, comment, or share anything about the matter, the parties, the witnesses, the mediator, or the Tribunal on any social-media platform — including Facebook, Instagram, X (Twitter), TikTok, LinkedIn, Reddit, or any blog. Posts can be subpoenaed, can damage credibility, and can be cited against the Client at the hearing.

18. No Action Without Funding

The Paralegal is not obliged to commence, continue, or complete any step (including filing the Application, attending mediation, or attending the hearing) unless the Client's account is current and any required deposit has been received in our trust account. The Client agrees that delay caused by non-payment is at the Client's risk, including the loss of limitation rights under s. 34 of the Code.

19. Withdrawal & Termination

The Client may terminate this retainer at any time by written notice. The Paralegal may withdraw on reasonable notice for cause permitted by the Paralegal Rules of Conduct, including non-payment of accounts, loss of confidence, conflict of interest, or breach of Section 11 (Client Conduct). On termination, the Client remains responsible for fees and disbursements incurred to the date of termination and for any reasonable file-transfer costs.

20. Indemnity & Liability

The Paralegal carries professional liability insurance as required by the Law Society of Ontario. Except as required by law, the Paralegal's aggregate liability under this Agreement is limited to the amount of professional liability insurance available to respond to the claim. This clause does not limit liability for fraud, willful misconduct, or any liability that cannot be excluded under the Paralegal Rules of Conduct.

21. Electronic Communications

The Client consents to receiving documents and communications by email. The Client acknowledges that email is not perfectly secure and that, despite reasonable precautions, communications may be intercepted, lost, or delayed. The Client agrees to advise the Paralegal of any change to the email address on file.
