2. Nature of the Matter & Definitions

What “cash for keys” means

“Cash for keys” (also called a “buy‑out” or “move‑out incentive”) is a private, negotiated payment from the Landlord to the Tenant in exchange for the Tenant’s voluntary surrender of possession of the rental unit and a mutual release of all claims arising out of the tenancy. The bargain is normally executed by a written agreement variously called a:

  • Mutual Release and Termination Agreement, or
  • Form N11 — Agreement to End the Tenancy together with a written side agreement recording the cash payment and mutual release.

What cash for keys is NOT

Cash for Keys Is a Private Deal — NOT an LTB Order

A cash-for-keys settlement is not an order of the Landlord and Tenant Board. It is not supervised, approved, or enforced by the Board. It is not a Board‑mediated settlement. It is a private contract between the Tenant and the Landlord. If the Landlord refuses to honour the agreement after signing, enforcement may require separate Small Claims Court proceedings, which are not included in this Retainer (see Section 4).

Why landlords pay cash for keys

Landlords offer cash for keys for a variety of reasons — including, for example, where they wish to renovate, demolish, convert, or sell the unit, or where they wish to avoid the time, cost, and risk of an LTB application. The Paralegal makes no representation about the Landlord’s motivation, and the Tenant should not assume the Landlord has any particular legal entitlement to possession.

Definitions used in this Retainer

  • “Settlement” means a written, signed cash-for-keys agreement (Mutual Release / Termination Agreement, or an N11 with a written side agreement) between the Tenant and the Landlord.
  • “Settlement Proceeds” means the cash actually paid by the Landlord to the Tenant (or to Legal Assist’s trust account on the Tenant’s behalf) under the Settlement.
  • “Floor” means the minimum dollar amount the Tenant has set as the lowest acceptable Settlement (see Section 5).

3. Scope of Retainer (Strictly Limited)

Limited Scope — Rule 3.02(1.1)

This Retainer is strictly limited to negotiating a cash-for-keys settlement between the Tenant and the Landlord. Nothing more.

This is a limited-scope retainer under Rule 3.02(1.1) of the Paralegal Rules of Conduct. By signing, the Tenant acknowledges that the Paralegal’s services are limited to the narrow scope set out below and that the Tenant remains responsible for everything else (including any LTB proceeding and any court matter), subject to retaining a paralegal or lawyer separately.

What this Retainer DOES include:

  • Communications with the Landlord and the Landlord’s representatives (property manager, real-estate agent, lawyer or paralegal acting for the Landlord) for the purpose of negotiating a cash-for-keys settlement.
  • Drafting initial settlement offers and counter-offers on behalf of the Tenant; reviewing offers and counter-offers received from the Landlord; and advising the Tenant on the strengths and weaknesses of each offer.
  • Drafting or reviewing the final Mutual Release and Termination Agreement (or N11 with a written side agreement) recording the agreed settlement, the agreed move-out date, the mutual release of all tenancy claims, and any other agreed terms.
  • Coordinating the mechanics of payment (deposit of Settlement Proceeds into Legal Assist’s trust account, or direct payment to the Tenant, as agreed) and the mechanics of move-out (return of keys, walk-through, utility shut-off coordination if applicable).
  • One (1) follow-up with the Landlord or the Landlord’s representative after signing the Settlement to confirm payment is in transit.

The detailed list of services that this Retainer does NOT include — including all LTB representation — appears in Section 4 (Scope Exclusions).

4. Scope Exclusions (What this Retainer Does NOT Cover)

Not Included — Read Carefully

This Retainer does NOT authorize Legal Assist to do any of the following on the Tenant’s behalf:

LTB representation — categorically excluded:

  • Any representation at the Landlord and Tenant Board — no hearing attendance, no Case Management Hearing, no mediation attendance, no merits hearing, no application filing or response, no review motion under RTA s. 21.2 / LTB Rule 26.
  • Drafting, filing, or responding to any LTB application, including but not limited to: L1 (non-payment of rent), L2 (termination/non-payment), L3 (uncontested termination), L4 (breach of mediated settlement), L9 (collect rent only), L10 (former tenant arrears), T1 (rebate), T2 (tenant rights), T3 (rent reduction), T5 (bad-faith N12/N13), T6 (maintenance), T7 (suite meter).
  • Drafting, reviewing, serving, or responding to any LTB notice, including but not limited to: N4 (non-payment), N5 (interference/damage), N6 (illegal act), N7 (serious impairment), N8 (persistent late payment), N9 (tenant termination), N12 (landlord own use), N13 (demolition/conversion/repairs). The Tenant remains solely responsible for responding to any such notice within the strict statutory deadlines (some as short as 5 days).
  • Defending against an eviction at the LTB, including but not limited to seeking relief from eviction under RTA s. 83.
  • Enforcement of an LTB order or filing for the Sheriff / Court Enforcement Office.

Other matters — categorically excluded:

  • Negotiating any matter unrelated to the cash-for-keys settlement, including but not limited to: rent abatement, repairs, harassment claims, T2 claims, T6 maintenance claims, return of last-month-rent deposit interest, illegal additional charges, or rent overpayments. If the Tenant believes any such claim exists, those claims should be discussed with the Paralegal separately and may require a separate retainer; otherwise, those claims may be released forever by signing the final Mutual Release (see Section 9).
  • Court proceedings of any kind, including without limitation Small Claims Court (e.g., to enforce a signed Settlement the Landlord refuses to honour), the Superior Court of Justice, Divisional Court appeals or judicial review under RTA s. 210, and the Court of Appeal.
  • Tax, accounting, or financial advice regarding the Settlement Proceeds (including possible effect on income tax, GST/HST, employment insurance, ODSP, Ontario Works, RGI subsidies, or any other government benefit).
  • Immigration advice regarding loss of residence, change of address with IRCC, or related matters.
  • Independent legal advice on the final Mutual Release. The Paralegal can draft and explain the Mutual Release but does not serve as “independent” counsel for the Tenant; the Tenant is strongly advised to obtain independent legal advice from a lawyer or a different paralegal before signing the final Mutual Release (see Section 9).
  • Locating alternative housing, moving services, storage, or any logistical support related to the Tenant’s move-out beyond the limited coordination described in Section 3.

9. Independent Legal Advice (ILA)

You Are Strongly Encouraged to Get Independent Legal Advice Before Signing the Mutual Release

The Tenant is strongly encouraged to obtain independent legal advice from a lawyer or another paralegal (one who is not acting on this Retainer) before signing the final Mutual Release and Termination Agreement (or N11 with side agreement). The Paralegal acting on this Retainer drafted or reviewed the document for the Tenant and therefore cannot serve as “independent” counsel on it.

Why ILA matters here

A Mutual Release is a final, full, and forever release of all claims the Tenant has, or may ever have, arising out of the tenancy. By signing it, the Tenant is normally giving up any right to bring claims that the Tenant could otherwise have pursued at the Landlord and Tenant Board (e.g., T2 harassment / interference claims, T6 maintenance claims, T1 rebate claims for illegal charges or LMR deposit interest, T3 service-reduction claims) or in court (e.g., damages for breach of the tenancy agreement). Independent legal advice helps ensure that the Tenant understands what claims are being released and whether the Settlement amount is reasonable in the circumstances.

When ILA is especially important

  • Where the Settlement amount is $5,000 or more;
  • Where the Tenant has, or may have, existing claims against the Landlord (e.g., a maintenance dispute, a harassment complaint, illegal charges, withheld LMR deposit interest) that would be released by signing;
  • Where the Tenant has, or may have, a potential bad-faith N12 or N13 claim (RTA s. 57) against the Landlord that would be released;
  • Where the Tenant is being asked to sign any term other than the cash payment, the move-out date, and a standard mutual release (e.g., a non-disparagement clause, a confidentiality clause, an indemnity).

Acknowledgement of right to ILA

By signing this Retainer, the Tenant acknowledges that the Tenant has been informed of the right to obtain independent legal advice before signing the final Mutual Release, and that the Tenant either has obtained, will obtain, or voluntarily chooses not to obtain such advice.

10. Termination of Retainer

Termination by the Tenant

The Tenant may terminate this Retainer at any time, for any reason or no reason, by giving the Paralegal written notice (email is sufficient). Termination by the Tenant is effective on the date the notice is received by the Paralegal. The fee consequences of termination by the Tenant are set out in Section 6.

Termination by the Paralegal

The Paralegal may terminate this Retainer on five (5) business days’ written notice to the Tenant, for any reason permitted by the Paralegal Rules of Conduct, including without limitation: non-cooperation, failure to provide instructions, conflict of interest identified after engagement, breakdown of trust, threatening or abusive conduct, ethical impossibility, or any other reason permitted under Rule 3.08 of the Rules.

Automatic Termination

This Retainer terminates automatically, without notice and without any further act of either party, on the date the Landlord (a) serves the Tenant with any LTB notice (N4, N5, N6, N7, N8, N12, N13, or any other N-form), OR (b) files any application at the Landlord and Tenant Board, OR (c) commences any court proceeding against the Tenant arising out of the tenancy. On automatic termination:

  • The Paralegal will confirm the termination in writing to the Tenant within one (1) business day of becoming aware of the triggering event.
  • The fee consequences set out in Section 6 apply.
  • If the Tenant wishes to be represented in respect of the LTB or court matter, the Tenant must sign a new, separate retainer with Legal Assist Paralegal Services (or with another paralegal or lawyer). The Paralegal will, on request, provide a referral.

Effect of Termination

On termination (for any reason), the Paralegal will (a) cease all work on the file, (b) close out any pending communications with the Landlord with notice that the Paralegal no longer acts, (c) on the Tenant’s written request and within a reasonable time, transfer the Tenant’s file (or a copy) to the Tenant or to a successor representative, and (d) issue a final account if any amount is owing under Section 6.
