4. Scope of Services (Included vs. Excluded)

This retainer covers the services in the “Included” column below for the specific charge(s) identified in Section 1. Items in the “Not Included” column require a separate written retainer.

Included in this retainer

  • Review of the Offence Notice and prosecution disclosure
  • Filing the Notice of Intention to Appear within 15 days
  • Disclosure request and review
  • First appearance and procedural attendances
  • Early Resolution Meeting or prosecutor pre-trial
  • Plea negotiations on Client instructions
  • Trial preparation and witness preparation
  • One (1) trial date in the Ontario Court of Justice
  • Section 11 reopening application on Form 102, where available
  • Routine client communication throughout the matter

Not included (separate retainer)

  • Appeals under Part VII or to the Court of Appeal (s. 139)
  • Criminal Code driving charges
  • MTO suspension review or driver-improvement interviews
  • Insurance disputes, rate or coverage appeals
  • Civil claims arising from the incident
  • Judicial review
  • Additional trial dates beyond the first
  • Client-caused adjournments
  • Reopening motions beyond the initial Form 102
  • Extraordinary or last-minute attendances outside ordinary practice

Unless expanded in writing, the flat-fee or hybrid fee selected under Section 6 covers all work up to and including one (1) trial date. Adjournments caused by the Client, additional trial dates, appeals, reopening motions beyond the initial Form 102 application, or extraordinary attendances are billed separately.

Photo radar / Automated Speed Enforcement

If the matter is an automated-speed-enforcement (photo-radar) ticket, liability attaches to the registered owner of the plate — there are no demerit points and no insurance-record impact. 5. Excluded Services & Collateral Consequences

In addition to the items in the Section 4 table, this retainer does not include ongoing monitoring, advice on, or representation in respect of:

  • Driver’s licence reinstatement, MTO administrative hearings, or suspension reviews;
  • Roadside Administrative Driver’s Licence Suspensions under HTA s. 48.3 or s. 172 — these run by operation of law and are not subject to plea negotiation;
  • Insurance underwriting, rate-setting, cancellation, non-renewal, or coverage disputes;
  • Commercial Vehicle Operator’s Registration (CVOR) consequences for commercial drivers or fleet operators;
  • Immigration consequences of a conviction (admissibility, work-permit, sponsorship);
  • Employment consequences (employer disclosure, driver-licence-condition jobs, professional regulatory disclosure);
  • Post-disposition enforcement issues (collections, plate-renewal blocks, default-fine matters) other than as specifically retained;
  • Criminal Code driving offences (e.g. impaired driving s. 320.14, dangerous driving s. 320.13).

6.1 Two-Phase Tiered Structure

The fees for this Provincial Offences matter are structured in two phases. The Paralegal first works to resolve the charge before trial (Phase 1). The Phase 2 fee is payable only if the matter does not resolve in Phase 1 and proceeds to a contested trial in the Ontario Court of Justice.

Phase 1

Pre-Trial Resolution

${RETAINER_DEPOSIT} + HST

Covers all work to attempt resolution before trial — document review, file preparation, and negotiations with the Crown prosecutor’s office to seek withdrawal, amendment, or reduction of the charge:

  • Review of the Offence Notice and prosecution disclosure
  • File preparation and analysis of the Crown’s evidence
  • Filing the Notice of Intention to Appear within the 15-day window
  • Disclosure request and review
  • Negotiations with the Crown prosecutor’s office to attempt reduction, amendment, or withdrawal of the charge
  • Early Resolution Meeting or prosecutor pre-trial
  • Routine client communication and strategy advice throughout Phase 1
Phase 2

Trial Representation

$500.00 + HST

Payable only if Phase 1 does not resolve the matter and the file proceeds to a contested trial. Covers everything associated with trial representation:

  • Trial preparation and witness preparation
  • One (1) trial date in the Ontario Court of Justice (Provincial Offences)
  • Cross-examination of the issuing officer and Crown witnesses
  • Submissions and argument on conviction and, if necessary, sentence
  • Post-disposition client briefing and explanation of the outcome

Phase 2 Is Additional to Phase 1

The Phase 2 fee of $500.00 + HST is in addition to the Phase 1 fee already paid; it is not a replacement, credit, or roll-over of Phase 1. If the charge is resolved in Phase 1 (withdrawal, amendment to a non-record offence, agreed plea, or other pre-trial disposition), the Phase 2 fee is not charged.

Additional trial dates beyond the first, appeals, reopening motions, and extraordinary attendances are not included in the Phase 2 fee and are billed separately under Section 6 above.

What the flat fee covers. Where the Flat Fee model is selected, the fee covers all work up to and including one trial date unless otherwise stated in writing in this Agreement.

Additional fees apply for: additional trial dates, adjournments caused by the Client (including non-attendance, late provision of information, scheduling conflicts, or changes of instruction), appeals, reopening motions beyond the initial Form 102 application, or extraordinary attendances. The Paralegal will provide written notice of any additional fee before incurring it where reasonably possible.

Adjournment costs. If the matter is adjourned due to the Client’s conduct, non-attendance, late disclosure of information, scheduling conflicts, or change of instructions, additional fees may apply.

Disbursements (court filing or transcript fees, courier, driver’s abstract or MTO record, witness expenses, document production, photocopying) are billed at cost. The Client authorizes the Paralegal to incur reasonable disbursements necessary for the conduct of the matter.

Payment terms. Invoices are due on receipt. Interest may be charged on overdue accounts at 2% per month (24% annually).

Refunds. Fees paid before a trial date has commenced are refundable to the extent the work has not been performed and is not committed. Once a trial or contested hearing has commenced, fees earned are non-refundable except where required by law or by the Paralegal Rules of Conduct.

Contingency fee arrangements are generally not appropriate for Provincial Offences matters because there is no monetary recovery.

8. Demerit Points, Insurance & Outcome Disclaimer

The Client acknowledges that the Paralegal does not control the prosecutor, the Court, the Ministry of Transportation, or the Client’s automobile insurer. The Paralegal does not promise or guarantee any particular outcome, including:

  • Withdrawal or reduction of the charge;
  • Avoidance of demerit points or driver-record entries;
  • Avoidance of an insurance-rate increase, cancellation, or non-renewal;
  • Avoidance of a fine, surcharge, or victim-fine surcharge;
  • Avoidance of a driver’s licence suspension where one applies by operation of law (for example, HTA s. 172 stunt driving carries a 30-day Administrative Driver’s Licence Suspension and 14-day vehicle impound that attach at the roadside regardless of plea outcome).

Insurance — no prediction. The Paralegal cannot predict or guarantee how any insurer will assess the charge, conviction, amendment, withdrawal, plea, or final disposition for underwriting purposes. Insurance consequences depend on the insurer’s own rules, the Client’s driving history, and other factors outside the Paralegal’s control. Clients with insurance concerns are encouraged to seek written confirmation from their insurer or broker.

9. Plea Authority & Client Control

The Paralegal will conduct plea negotiations in accordance with the Client’s instructions. The decision whether to accept any plea offer or proposed resolution remains with the Client. The Paralegal will not enter a plea or accept a resolution without the Client’s express authorization, except where the Client has provided prior written settlement authority.

The Client acknowledges that paying the set fine on the back of the Offence Notice (Option 1) is a plea of guilty under the Provincial Offences Act and disposes of the charge. The Client agrees not to take that step without consulting the Paralegal.

The Client agrees to preserve and promptly provide all potentially relevant evidence, including dashcam footage, surveillance footage, photographs, witness contact information, and a written recollection of the events. Destruction or alteration of evidence may negatively affect the proceeding.

The Paralegal does not create evidence and cannot guarantee a successful outcome where evidence is incomplete, unavailable, inconsistent, or weak.

13. Hearing Attendance & Virtual Proceedings

The Paralegal will attend on the Client’s behalf where the Provincial Offences Act permits a paralegal to appear without the defendant. The Client must be available by phone for the duration of any appearance, and must attend in person where the Court requires the defendant’s personal attendance (for example, certain Part III trials or where the Court so orders). Failure to attend, where attendance is required, may result in conviction in absentia, a bench warrant in serious matters, or other adverse consequences.

Virtual hearings. Provincial Offences hearings may occur virtually by Zoom, audio conference, or other remote technology as directed by the Court. The Client agrees to ensure access to a quiet location, a working device, and a stable internet connection for any scheduled virtual appearance.

14. Client Conduct & Anti-Harassment

The Client agrees to behave courteously toward the Paralegal, firm personnel, Court staff, witnesses, opposing counsel, and the prosecutor. Threatening, harassing, abusive, or discriminatory conduct directed at any of the foregoing — whether in person, by email, by phone, or online — will not be tolerated and is grounds for the Paralegal to withdraw from the retainer under Section 23.

15. Recording, Witnesses & Media

Ontario is a one-party-consent jurisdiction for the recording of conversations. Without limiting any lawful right to record, the Client agrees:

  • Not to publish, disseminate, or misuse recordings of communications with the Paralegal, firm personnel, witnesses, or litigation participants;
  • To be aware that recordings of privileged communications may impair or waive privilege, and the Client is discouraged from recording privileged communications without notice to the Paralegal;
  • Not to contact the prosecutor, the issuing officer, the Court, or potential witnesses directly without the Paralegal’s knowledge;
  • Not to send communications that undermine legal strategy;
  • Not to communicate with media outlets, journalists, or commentators regarding the proceeding without first consulting the Paralegal.

16. Settlement

21. Online & Social-Media Conduct

Do Not Post — and Do Not Delete

The Client agrees not to post, comment, or share anything about the charge, the underlying incident, the officer, or the proceeding on any social-media or online platform. Posts can be subpoenaed, can damage credibility, and can be cited against the Client at trial.

The Client is also instructed not to delete existing online content that may relate to the proceeding without first consulting the Paralegal. Deletion can itself amount to spoliation of evidence.

23. Withdrawal, File Abandonment & Termination

The Client may terminate this retainer at any time by written notice. The Paralegal may withdraw on reasonable notice for cause permitted by the Paralegal Rules of Conduct, including non-payment of accounts, loss of confidence, conflict of interest, inability to obtain instructions, the Client insisting on a dishonest or unethical course of conduct, or breach of Section 14. The Paralegal may also withdraw where continued representation would be unethical, impractical, or inconsistent with professional obligations.

File abandonment. If the Client becomes unreachable for more than sixty (60) days despite reasonable efforts by the Paralegal to contact the Client, the Paralegal may close the file, subject to professional obligations and any imminent court deadlines. Fees and disbursements incurred to the date of closure remain payable.

On termination, the Client remains responsible for fees and disbursements incurred to the date of termination and for any reasonable file-transfer costs.
