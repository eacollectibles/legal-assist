4. Scope of Services

Subject to Section 5, our services include:

  • Reviewing the facts and assessing whether the matter is governed by the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (the “RTA”) and within the LTB’s jurisdiction;
  • Reviewing the tenancy agreement (including the Ontario Standard Lease, Form 2229), prior notices, ledgers, and correspondence;
  • Filing tenant applications on the appropriate form (T1, T2, T3, T5, T6, T7) and serving the landlord per LTB Rules;
  • Defending landlord‑initiated applications (L1, L2, L3, L4, L9) where the Client has been named as a tenant respondent;
  • Responding to landlord notices (N4 non‑payment of rent, N5 substantial interference/damage, N6 illegal act, N7 serious impairment, N8 persistent late payment, N12 landlord’s own use, N13 demolition/conversion/repairs) and advising on cure/void periods under RTA ss. 59, 62, 64;
  • Preparing and exchanging disclosure under LTB Rules;
  • Drafting written submissions, adjournment requests, and procedural correspondence;
  • Representing the Client at the Case Management Hearing (CMH), at LTB mediation, and at the merits hearing (virtual unless ordered in‑person);
  • Negotiating settlements and drafting Mediated Settlement Agreements or consent orders;
  • Where applicable, requesting relief from eviction under RTA s. 83 and presenting circumstances supporting such relief;
  • Receiving and explaining the LTB’s order under RTA s. 197;
  • Advising on next steps, including the right to request a Review under RTA s. 21.2 (LTB Rule 26.8) within 30 days of the order.

13. Limitation & Notice Periods

Tenant Applications — One-Year Limit

Under RTA s. 29(2), a tenant application generally must be filed within one year after the conduct or event complained of. For T6 maintenance issues, the one‑year clock runs from the most recent breach in the pattern of conduct. For T1 last‑month‑rent rebate claims after the tenancy has ended, additional rules apply.

Defending an Eviction — Days, Not Months

If the Client has been served a landlord notice (N4, N5, N6, N7, N8, N12, N13), the compliance, void, or notice period may be as short as 5 days. The notice itself is not an order — the landlord must still file at the LTB and obtain a hearing. The Paralegal will identify all applicable deadlines on intake.

The Client confirms that they have advised the Paralegal of every notice received and every relevant date, and that the Application or Response can be filed within the applicable period, or that the Client is aware of the risks where a period is approaching or has passed.

14. Case Management Hearing (CMH) & Mediation

Many LTB matters are scheduled for a Case Management Hearing (CMH) before the merits hearing. A CMH is conducted by a Board mediator or member to attempt resolution and, where unresolved, to identify the issues, schedule the hearing, and direct disclosure. The Paralegal will attend the CMH with the Client.

If a settlement is reached at mediation or at the CMH, the parties typically sign a Mediated Settlement Agreement that is enforceable as an LTB order. Discussions, offers, and admissions made in mediation are without prejudice and are not admissible at the merits hearing if mediation fails, except for the final, signed written settlement and to the extent disclosure is otherwise required by law.

17. No Self‑Help, Rent Withholding or Repair‑and‑Deduct

Critical Tenant Warning

Ontario law does not permit a tenant to unilaterally withhold rent, deduct repair costs from rent, or hire a contractor and bill the landlord, even where the landlord is in breach of the RTA. Unilateral withholding is the single most common reason that tenants with valid complaints end up evicted.

The Client agrees:

  • To continue paying rent in full and on time for the duration of this retainer, including during any LTB proceeding;
  • Not to deduct any amount from rent (including for repairs, alleged overpayment, or set‑off) without first obtaining either (a) the landlord’s written consent, or (b) a written LTB order authorizing the deduction;
  • Not to hire contractors and demand reimbursement from the landlord without prior written authorization;
  • Not to vacate the unit without first consulting the Paralegal, as moving out can defeat or limit several remedies available under the RTA.

Where the Client believes a remedy such as rent abatement or a repair order is appropriate, the proper procedure is to apply to the LTB on the correct form (typically T2 or T6) and seek the remedy from the Board.
